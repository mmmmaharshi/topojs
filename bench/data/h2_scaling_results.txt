H2 SCALING SWEEP -- "standard" (plain) engine
Generated by: npm run bench:h2-scaling (node --experimental-strip-types bench/h2-scaling.ts)
Real data: monthly sunspot counts (SIDC/WDC-SILSO), 2D delay embedding (lag=6),
maxDist=0.1, maxDim=3 (tetrahedra construction = H2).

PURPOSE. README.md's "Against Ripser" section previously stated "H2 doesn't
finish at n=400 in the plain engine" without ever locating exactly where the
wall is, or whether it's a hard crash (memory/stack) or a soft wall (still
correct, just slow beyond practical patience). This sweep answers both.

RESULTS:
      n   tetrahedra   triangles   edges         ms       status
     50         1633         909     308        7.0       ok
    100        81280       11505    1316      346.5       ok
    150       171630       25938    2701     1120.9       ok
    200       556298       64442    4974     9922.5       ok
    225       994700       97339    6429    30758.7       BUDGET EXCEEDED (>20s)

GROWTH RATE (log-log slope, n=50 to n=225): tetrahedra ~ n^4.9, time ~ n^5.6.
(Tetrahedra count growing close to the combinatorial C(n,4) ~ n^4 worst case
is expected for 4-cliques in a moderately dense Rips complex at this maxDist;
time growing faster than tetrahedra count reflects the matrix-reduction cost
per tetrahedron also growing with complex size, not staying constant.)

ANSWER TO "IS IT TIME OR STRUCTURAL": time. No crash, no memory error, no
exception at any n tested -- computePersistentHomology with engine="standard"
and maxDim=3 returns a correct result at every n up to 225; it simply takes
30.8 seconds at n=225 and would take on the order of several minutes by
n=400 at this growth rate (extrapolating n^5.6: (400/225)^5.6 ≈ 10.4x →
~320s estimated, not measured directly -- flagged as an extrapolation, not
a fresh measurement). This is a SOFT wall: the plain engine's explicit
tetrahedra enumeration + full boundary-matrix reduction becomes impractical
well before n=400, starting around n=200-225 for this maxDist/dataset
combination, not a hard failure at exactly n=400.

CORRECTION TO README.md's PRIOR WORDING. "H2 doesn't finish at n=400" reads
as a hard limit; the measured reality is a combinatorial time blowup that
becomes impractical starting around n=200-225 (already 10-30 seconds per
call at that size, for this maxDist), well before n=400 -- n=400 was simply
where an earlier, unrelated timing test (bench/compare_ripser.py's own
40-second subprocess budget) happened to give up, not a special threshold in
the algorithm itself. README.md has been corrected to reflect the measured
growth curve instead of the single anecdotal cutoff.
