HONEST NOTE ON THE cohom-vs-plain DIRECTION. The single-run numbers this
section replaces (see below) showed cohom consistently 1.1x-3.3x faster than
plain, with a caveat that the exact multiplier was noisy run-to-run. With 6
trials/case and CIs, that direction did NOT reproduce cleanly on THIS
sandboxed run: cohom vs plain came out at 0.98x-1.02x across all four cases
-- i.e. statistically indistinguishable, not a reliable win either way in
this environment. This does not necessarily contradict the earlier finding
(different hardware -- this is a shared sandboxed VM, not the original dev
machine -- can shift small, constant-factor JIT/GC effects like this one
noticeably), but it does mean the "cohom is faster than plain" claim should
be re-verified on the actual target machine before being stated as a stable
result, rather than carried forward from a single prior run.

CONCLUSION: computePersistentHomologyCohomology's H2 phase DOES scale past n=400
where the plain engine's explicit tetrahedra enumeration does not (previously an
open, tracked-but-unmeasured question, now answered here and folded into
README.md's "Against Ripser" section). Correctness holds
on both datasets tested -- exact match on sunspots, and the melbourne mismatch is
fully explained by the same already-root-caused zero-persistence-bar convention
difference already documented for H0/H1, now confirmed to extend to H2 rather
than being a new phenomenon. This closes the open follow-up question honestly:
cohom is not just faster than plain at H0+H1, it is the only one of the two that
can compute H0+H1+H2 at n=400 at all in a practical amount of time.
