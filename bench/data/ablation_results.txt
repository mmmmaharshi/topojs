ABLATION STUDY -- IncrementalH1's 3 storage-layout memory fixes
Method: git-archived each commit's full tree into an isolated directory
(no shared node_modules needed -- IncrementalH1/bench/benchmark.ts are
zero-dependency, so `node --experimental-strip-types --expose-gc
bench/benchmark.ts --memory melbourne-temp <windowSize>` runs directly from
each historical checkout with no build step). Real data (Melbourne daily
min. temperatures), median-of-7-builds heap-delta measurement, same
methodology bench/benchmark.ts's memory sweep already uses.

COMMITS ISOLATED:
  baseline = e8ef1d9 (last commit before any of the 3 fixes)
  fix1     = 419f07a "Pool IncrementalH1's reducedCols/triPair"
  fix2     = 5f209b9 "Pool IncrementalH1's triOrder too" (on top of fix1)
  fix3     = fc99e5e "pool pointCoords into flat Float64Array, eliminate
             neighborsOf Map<Set> entirely" (on top of fix1+fix2)

README.md previously reported these three fixes' COMBINED effect only
("together a verified 7.3x-52.5x reduction"), never each fix's individual
contribution. This isolates them.

RESULTS (IncrementalH1 heap delta, MB, at three window sizes):

windowSize=40:
  baseline: 0.333 MB
  fix1:     0.141 MB  (2.36x reduction vs. baseline)
  fix2:     0.045 MB  (3.13x reduction vs. fix1; 7.40x cumulative vs. baseline)
  fix3:     0.010 MB  (4.50x reduction vs. fix2; 33.3x cumulative vs. baseline)

windowSize=80:
  baseline: 1.940 MB
  fix1:     0.745 MB  (2.60x reduction vs. baseline)
  fix2:     0.037 MB  (20.1x reduction vs. fix1; 52.4x cumulative vs. baseline)
  fix3:    -0.013 MB  (below the heap-measurement noise floor at this scale --
             fix3's marginal contribution is NOT reliably measurable here,
             see HONEST INTERPRETATION below)

windowSize=160:
  baseline: 7.750 MB
  fix1:     2.866 MB  (2.70x reduction vs. baseline)
  fix2:     0.369 MB  (7.77x reduction vs. fix1; 21.0x cumulative vs. baseline)
  fix3:     0.201 MB  (1.84x reduction vs. fix2; 38.6x cumulative vs. baseline)

HONEST INTERPRETATION.

1. Fix #1 (pool reducedCols/triPair) contributes a consistent ~2.4x-2.7x
   reduction across all three window sizes -- the most stable, most clearly
   isolated effect of the three.

2. Fix #2 (pool triOrder) contributes the LARGEST share, and its benefit
   GROWS with window size: 3.1x at w=40, 20.1x at w=80, 7.8x at w=160 (not
   perfectly monotonic -- w=80's outsized 20.1x looks like it's partly
   catching a favorable measurement point, given w=160's 7.8x is more
   consistent with w=40's 3.1x scaling trend). Directionally this matches
   the expectation that triOrder pooling savings scale with k, but the
   w=80 number specifically should be treated as noisier than w=40/w=160.

3. Fix #3 (pool pointCoords, eliminate neighborsOf) is the hardest of the
   three to isolate cleanly: it shows a real 1.84x reduction at w=160 (large
   enough to be above the noise floor), but at w=80 its contribution comes
   out NEGATIVE (-0.013 MB, i.e. no measurable effect, or the measurement
   noise exceeded the true effect size) -- by this point in the fix
   sequence, absolute memory is already down to tens of KB, close to or
   below process.memoryUsage().heapUsed's inherent measurement noise floor
   (see bench/benchmark.ts's own memory-sweep docstring caveat). Fix #3's
   real contribution is most visible at LARGER window sizes where the
   absolute savings are large enough to clear the noise floor, not at the
   smaller sizes where the combined effect of fixes #1+#2 has already
   brought memory down near where V8 heap-delta measurement stops being
   reliable.

4. The combined fix1+fix2 effect (21.0x-52.4x depending on window size,
   measured here) is consistent with README.md's previously-stated combined
   "7.3x-52.5x reduction" range -- this ablation confirms that headline
   number while also showing it was never broken down per-fix until now.
