COMPLEXITY FORMULA FIT
Generated by: npm run bench:complexity-fit (node --experimental-strip-types bench/complexity-fit.ts)

PURPOSE. incremental-h1.ts's docstring states IncrementalH1's per-push cost
is Theta(E+T) + O(k) + O(deg(new)^2), used to EXPLAIN observed behavior
(e.g. "O(deg(new)^2) eventually dominates the O(k) term at larger windows")
but never fit against measured per-push timings. This records (E, T, k,
deg(new)) alongside wall-clock time for individual real-data pushes across a
range of window sizes and fits an OLS model, reporting R^2 for the full
3-term model plus single-term alternatives.

HONEST INTERPRETATION.

1. The overall functional form is well supported: the full model explains
   98.2%-98.8% of variance in per-push time. This is strong empirical
   support for "time scales with realized complex size, not a flat
   function of k alone" -- the paper's actual comparative point against
   the naive engine.

2. (E+T) ALONE is the dominant, cleanly-isolable predictor: 93.0%-96.6% of
   variance by itself, barely below the full 3-term model. Adding k and
   deg(new)^2 only improves R^2 by 1.6-5.8 percentage points.

3. Individual coefficients for k and deg(new)^2 in the full model are NOT
   reliably interpretable as "the O(k) term's contribution" or "the
   O(deg(new)^2) term's contribution" in isolation -- both come out
   NEGATIVE in the fitted model (e.g. k's coefficient is -0.343 for
   sunspots), which would wrongly suggest larger windows independently
   REDUCE time if read literally. The real explanation is multicollinearity:
   k, E+T, and deg(new)^2 are all substantially correlated with each other
   in real windowed data (corr(k, E+T) = 0.84-0.95, corr(k, deg^2) =
   0.64-0.73, corr(E+T, deg^2) = 0.70-0.79) because k mechanically
   determines the ceiling on E, T, and deg(new). OLS cannot cleanly
   separate three highly-correlated predictors' individual effects; only
   the combined model's overall fit is trustworthy here, not the per-term
   coefficient signs or magnitudes.

4. This does not contradict the derivation in incremental-h1.ts (which is a
   worst-case asymptotic argument, not a claim about linear separability of
   its terms) -- it does mean the docstring's three-term breakdown should be
   read as "the union of these effects explains observed timing well" rather
   than "each term's contribution is separately measurable via regression on
   real data", which real data's collinear structure makes infeasible
   without a controlled (non-real) design that varies k, E+T, and deg(new)
   independently.
